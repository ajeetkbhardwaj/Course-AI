## 1. Introduction to Depth Estimation

Depth estimation is the third major focus area of the Computer Vision module, shifting from semantic tasks (recognition/detection) to a **metric/measurement** task [[00:32](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk3MP4z55RM&t=32)].

### A. Goal

* To find the **depth for each pixel** in an image [[00:52](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk3MP4z55RM&t=52)].
* **Depth** is defined as the distance of an object from the camera position [[01:14](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk3MP4z55RM&t=74)].
* The goal is to output a depth map (or disparity map) with the same resolution as the input image [[01:01](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk3MP4z55RM&t=61)].

### B. Applications of Depth

Depth information is crucial for various real-world applications [[01:27](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk3MP4z55RM&t=87)]:

| **Application**                           | **Necessity of Depth**                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Navigation**                            | Robots, autonomous cars, and drones need to know the distance to objects to safely maneuver and avoid collisions [[01:33](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk3MP4z55RM&t=93)].                                                                                |
| **Robotic Surgery**                       | Needed to measure how far surgical instruments are from sensitive parts of the body [[01:54](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk3MP4z55RM&t=114)].                                                                                                            |
| **Augmented/Virtual Reality (AR/VR)**     | Required to correctly place virtual objects (augmentation) into the real world. For example, knowing the depth and surface normal of a table allows a virtual toy to be placed correctly on it [[02:16](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk3MP4z55RM&t=136)]. |
| **Scene Understanding/3D Reconstruction** | Necessary for capturing and reconstructing the 3D structure of heritage sites, sculptures, or general scenes [[02:45](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk3MP4z55RM&t=165)].                                                                                   |

## 2. Depth from Stereo Vision

The most common and geometrically robust method for obtaining depth is  **Stereo Vision** , which mimics how human eyes perceive depth [[03:14](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk3MP4z55RM&t=194)].

### A. The Principle of Stereo

* **Two Images:** Stereo vision uses two cameras (a left camera and a right camera) to capture two slightly offset images of the same scene [[03:21](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk3MP4z55RM&t=201)].
* **Disparity (Parallax):** Although the images look similar, the position of objects is slightly shifted between the two views. This shift, visible when superimposing the images (anaglyph), is called **disparity** or **parallax** [[03:40](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk3MP4z55RM&t=220)].
* **Depth Cue:** The magnitude of this disparity is inversely proportional to the depth: objects closer to the camera have a  **larger disparity** , while distant objects (like a tower) have a smaller disparity [[04:23](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk3MP4z55RM&t=263)]. The brain uses the disparity between the images captured by our two eyes to interpret depth [[04:50](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk3MP4z55RM&t=290)].

### B. The Triangulation Process

The process of calculating the 3D position (and thus depth) from two 2D images is called **triangulation** [[09:48](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk3MP4z55RM&t=588)].

1. **Image Projection (Forward):** A single 3D point **$P$** is projected through the **camera center** (**$O_1$** and **$O_2$**) onto the two image planes, creating two corresponding 2D points, **$p$** (left image) and **$p'$** (right image) [[05:34](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk3MP4z55RM&t=334)].
2. **Point Correspondence (Inference Step 1):** Given only the two input images, the first task is to solve the  **point correspondence problem** : For every pixel **$p$** in the left image, find the exact matching pixel **$p'$** in the right image that corresponds to the same 3D point **$P$** [[07:45](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk3MP4z55RM&t=465)].
3. **Triangulation (Inference Step 2):** Once the corresponding points (**$p$** and **$p'$**) are found:
   * **Draw a line** from the left camera center **$O_1$** through **$p$**.
   * **Draw a second line** from the right camera center **$O_2$** through **$p'$**.
   * The intersection of these two 3D lines gives the exact **3D location** (**$P$**) of the object, from which its depth can be calculated [[09:43](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk3MP4z55RM&t=583)].

### C. Limitation of Monocular (Single) Image Depth

If only a **single image** (monocular image) is available, depth cannot be determined using triangulation [[12:14](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk3MP4z55RM&t=734)].

* **Ambiguity:** Given a point **$p$** in the single image, all we know is that its corresponding 3D point **$P$** must lie somewhere along the 3D line connecting the camera center **$O$** and the image point **$p$** [[10:49](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk3MP4z55RM&t=649)].
* **Result:** Without a second image to provide a second constraint (the intersection point), the exact depth (distance along that line) is **ambiguous** [[12:07](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk3MP4z55RM&t=727)].

This leads to the next problem the course focuses on:  **Depth from a Single Image** , which relies on non-geometric cues to solve this ambiguity [[12:41](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk3MP4z55RM&t=761)].
